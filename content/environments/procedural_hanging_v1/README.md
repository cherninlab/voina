# Procedural hanging dressing v1 (T-164)

Banners, tapestries, cloth thrown over beams, cobwebs and sagging rope for the dungeon. Every
mesh is original geometry generated by `scripts/art/build_hanging.py` in Blender 5.2.2 with
`--factory-startup`; no third-party asset is used. Cloth is simulated offline with Blender's
cloth modifier and baked to a static mesh: nothing simulates at runtime and no collision is
exported. Placement in Red Descent belongs to T-162.

```sh
blender --factory-startup -b --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/art/build_hanging.py -- \
    content/environments/procedural_hanging_v1 \
    --review content/environments/red_descent/review/hanging_v1      # optional turntables
# --only banner,rope rebuilds some types and keeps the others' report entries; --seed N (164)
```

`bake.json` records, per asset: file and sha256, sockets and bounds in Godot coordinates relative
to the GLB origin, cloth size and grid, frames (300 at 30 fps), maximum pin error, maximum and
minimum edge length ratio against the rest grid, last-frame motion, a per-second motion trace,
solidify thickness, AO statistics, triangles, holes and tears. A rerun on this machine
reproduced all 18 GLBs byte for byte; another Blender build or CPU may settle folds a fraction
of a millimetre differently and change the hashes.

## Method

`scripts/art/procgen/cloth.py` (new toolkit module) builds a grid panel in metres, cuts a ragged
hem (noise, swallowtail or point shapes, notches), frays the sides, punches holes and slits tears
up from the hem, drops anything no longer hanging from a pin, erodes cells held by a single edge
(they flutter), smooths the cut outline and triangulates. Pins start on the rest grid and a
shape key eases them onto their sockets over the first 30 frames, so gathering a banner between
nails is part of the simulation. After 300 frames the evaluated mesh is measured and baked:

- pin error: settled pinned vertex to its socket. Blender hard-constrains full-weight pins to
  their goal, so this is zero to float precision; it guards against export and axis mistakes.
  The GLB socket nodes match bake.json to 0.000000 m in the Godot preview.
- edge stretch: settled / rest edge length, over every edge. Limit 1.05; worst 1.016 (drape_1).
- settle motion: largest vertex move over the last frame. Limit 1 mm. Webs and most banners
  settle to microns; drapes and the drooping or badly torn hangings keep up to 0.85 mm of
  jitter where cloth touches its beam proxy (worst tapestry_1).
- Cloth then gets a centred 4 mm solidify (pins stay on the mid surface, so the exported shell
  is 2 mm either side of each socket: `socket_surface_gap_m`) and AO baked by Cycles into
  Color.R with the unexported beam proxy present, so the nailed edge darkens.

Banners collide with their beam proxy; drapes fall onto a chamfered beam proxy (the dungeon's
hewn timber section) and are nailed twice on its top. Webs are pre-draped single-sided cards
settled under gravity. Ropes use the toolkit's `curves.catenary` and `curves.tube` (no
simulation): bound ends hide the tube openings, rope_2 has a hanging tail.

## Assets and sockets

Sockets are glTF nodes named `socket_<name>` in each GLB (Node3D after import) and are listed in
`bake.json` (`sockets_godot`). Godot axes: Y up.

| Type | Files | Origin and mounting | Sockets |
|---|---|---|---|
| banner | `banner_0..2.glb` (0.7x1.7 swallowtail, 1.0x2.4 with a lost nail and drooping corner, 0.55x1.3 pointed) | Midpoint of the outer nails. Nails on local X at y = 0, cloth hangs to -Y, front +Z. Put the origin on a beam underside (or a batten), local X along the beam. | `pin_0..n` |
| tapestry | `tapestry_0..2.glb` (1.8x1.3, 2.4x1.6 with the fourth nail lost, 1.4x1.9 badly torn) | As banner. On a wall, use a batten: cloth reaches 0.1-0.35 m behind the nail line. | `pin_0..n` |
| drape | `drape_0..2.glb` | Beam axis, beam along local X. Baked for the dungeon sections width x height: drape_0 0.25x0.25 (cloth bearer), drape_1 0.42x0.55 (roof tie), drape_2 0.30x0.38 (wall bearer). Only use on that section. | `nail_0`, `nail_1` (beam top) |
| web_corner | `web_corner_0..2.glb` | Apex at the origin. 0 and 1: anchored edges along +X and +Z under a ceiling where two walls meet. 2: edges along +X (beam underside) and -Y (post face), front +Z. | `apex`, `end_u`, `end_v` |
| web_sheet | `web_sheet_0..2.glb` | Midway between two parallel anchor lines along X, line A at z = -gap/2, line B at +gap/2 and `rise` lower: gap 1.26 (the clear gap between deck stringers), 0.7, 1.5 with 0.3 rise. Lines go on the beams' inner bottom arrises. | `a_0`, `a_1`, `b_0`, `b_1` |
| rope | `rope_0..2.glb` | Socket a at the origin, b at (span, rise, 0): 1.8 m level, 3.0 m dropping 0.6 m, 1.4 m with a 0.55 m tail. | `a`, `b` |

Cloth stays under 4,740 triangles (limit 6,000); webs 150-790; ropes 1,360-1,622.

## Materials

- `hanging_cloth.gdshader`, presets `cloth_crimson.tres`, `cloth_ochre.tres`, `cloth_ash.tres`,
  `rope_hemp.tres`: double sided (cull disabled) like the crypt's `cloth` material; plain weave
  from the metre UVs that fades before it aliases, faded dye, stains, bleached streaks, grime at
  the hem and on worn edges, a soft rim for wool fuzz. `rope = true` swaps the weave for a lay.
- `cobweb.gdshader`, presets `cobweb_corner.tres` (style 0) and `cobweb_sheet.tres` (style 1):
  an alpha-blended card with no texture. The silk is drawn procedurally from the metre UVs:
  style 0 fans radial threads from UV (0, 0), the corner apex, some snapped part way, crossed by
  broken straight capture threads between neighbouring radials, with loose strands and dust
  thickening into the corner; style 1 layers long, slightly bent, broken strands at random
  angles over a dusty film. Thread coverage is the silk width over the pixel footprint, so
  distant webs fade to haze instead of shimmering. Free edges thin out using Color.B.
- Vertex colour (COLOR_0): R = baked AO, G = per-asset variation, B = free-edge wear (1 at torn
  hems, holes and web edges). A MultiMesh instance colour multiplies COLOR, so keep it white
  when batching these meshes, as DungeonStructure does for `cloth`.

## Preview

`preview/hanging_preview.tscn` builds a stage from dungeon timber (`hewn_timber_0.glb` fitted as
`DungeonStructure.timber` does, with the crypt's wood material) under a warm OmniLight3D and
places every asset only from its bake.json sockets, asserting the GLB socket nodes agree.
`preview/capture.gd` saves Mobile/Metal frames of it; `preview/capture_in_dungeon.gd` hangs
banners, a tapestry, drape_1 and a corner web on the middle nave roof tie of the real Red Descent
at runtime (no gameplay file changes) with a capture-only lamp. Run both through
`scripts/godot_bg.sh` (see the script headers). Evidence: `../red_descent/review/hanging_v1/`.
