# Procedural character and heavy sword

Hold **left click** (or the rebound melee key, **F** by default) and drag at least **36 screen pixels** to start an attack. **Camera look continues normally throughout the drag and swing.** One press produces one damaging swing; release before the next attack. A click alone does not attack. The first click after Esc only recaptures the cursor.

The sword uses exaggerated angular inertia, and an approved cut is a torque rather than a launch velocity. The torque builds over **0.2 s**, so a level cut starts slowly (under 1 m/s at the tip), accelerates to its peak of roughly **13–14 m/s around 0.22 s**, then carries the rest of the way at 11–12 m/s on its momentum in a low arc (the tip rises to about hip height, roughly 1 m). It touches down after about **0.68 s, near 185 degrees**, and slides to rest at about **190 degrees** from where it started. A long drag adds steering and lift: about 195 degrees, with the tip at most about 1.4 m. A downward cut from an already raised blade chops with gravity; from the ground it first lifts. Mouse movement while held adds steering and lift, but only in proportion to how far the cut's torque has built, so a fast drag cannot skip the wind-up. **Stopping the mouse allows follow-through without pulling the blade back toward the camera.** Each gesture is limited to about 195 degrees of travel (tracked unwrapped, so it holds past half a turn), with braking near the end, so a held gesture cannot turn into an endless orbit. Release and press again to start the next damaging swing from the current blade position.

The same drag that fires a cut also turns the camera, so a live cut sweeps in the view's frame: the view's turn is carried into the blade and body at up to **6 rad/s**, ramped with the cut's torque, and capped at **2.4 rad** of view turn per cut. A swing therefore turns about **180-190 degrees on screen** however far the camera moved (a world-fixed sweep looked like only ~85 degrees after a 110-degree camera flick), and a circling drag still stops short of an orbit.

The owner's view sways slightly with the blade (`SwingCamera`): the blade's own angular speed, never mouse look, sets a small target turn (up to **~3 degrees**), bank (**~4 degrees**) and nod (**~2 degrees**) toward the cut. A soft, slightly underdamped spring follows it a beat behind the blade, so a level cut peaks around **2.5 degrees of turn and 3.5 degrees of bank**, then the view settles back within about **0.6 s** of the landing with a barely visible overshoot. Only the local camera moves; aim, the swing and other players' views are unaffected. The limits are constants at the top of `gameplay/player/swing_camera.gd`.

Heading and elevation are integrated separately from the hands and body. Moving the grip, stepping, or correcting the rod length cannot add energy to the blade. The blade has bounded angular speed and elevation, and ground contact dissipates its motion. These are deliberate game-feel constraints, not a free rigid-body simulation.

The torso follows the blade with a damped, capped turn. A cut gives the owner one short movement pulse in the launch direction, capped at **1.8 m/s** for a **0.6 s** envelope; it does not chase the rotating tip. This breaks the previous sword/body feedback loop that could produce spiralling. Lean emphasizes the pickup, lateral swing and downward impact. Looking without dragging leaves the sword's landing in place. Walking away first stretches the arms, then drags the grounded tip; resistance applies only while moving away from it. Walking onto the planted blade never folds the hilt back through the hips: the hands keep it at least **0.22 m** ahead on the hips-to-tip line and push the tip along the floor. Blocking deliberately raises the guard toward the view. The camera remains free throughout. The test beam input is disabled; Ctrl/right click blocks.

## Procedural pose

A `SkeletonModifier3D` generates the gameplay pose from the rig's rest transforms each frame:

- The hips stay dropped for a half-bent stance and lower further at speed; lean and bob respond to the sword and stepping state; the spine and head turn toward the view.
- Two-bone arm IK puts the wrists on the hilt in a hammer grip built from the rigs' measured hand axes: thumb side toward the blade, palm against the grip. The two grip points sit **10 cm** apart just below the guard, about a hand's width, so the hands stack together on the hilt. Both hands grip while dragging or while the blade stands ahead; a blade left off to one side is held by the hand on that side. The hilt is constrained to arm reach; fingers curl.
- Two-bone leg IK reaches world-space planted ankle targets. A planted foot steps once it trails the hips by half a stride and lands where the hips will be when the step ends, so feet keep up at a run instead of trailing; ground rays provide contact height and normal, and airborne legs tuck.

Imported clips remain available for asset inspection, but runtime locomotion and sword posing use the procedural solver. Both rigs share this behavior. The locally generated longsword rests at its last landing and stays visible in first person while the body keeps its shadows-only behavior.

The owner predicts locally. Host-approved attack direction provides the initial impulse; ongoing yaw, pitch and held intent travel with owner movement synchronization so peers reconstruct the motion. Held intent is cosmetic and cannot authorize damage. Ownership, finite direction validation, stamina, cooldown, phases, hit resolution and the existing rewind logic remain with the host.

## Raiders

The host sweeps each player's simulated blade against hostile raiders every physics tick while a swing is approved (`gameplay/player/blade_contact.gd`). The blade point that touches moves with the tip scaled by its position along the blade; above 3 m/s it shoves the raider along that direction with a hop and a lean, deals the swing's damage once per target, and above 10 m/s it bisects the raider: the mob dies and two rigid halves, clipped by a plane through the contact that contains the blade and its travel, tumble away with exposed flesh. This is host-only and cosmetic between players; see [mobs](mobs.md).

## Tuning

Release the cursor with Esc and open **Sword tweaks** (below the Controls button) for live sliders over the angular motion: gesture drive and damping, body follow and turn cap, cut speed (the angular speed a level cut builds up to), air damping, gravity, mouse lift, floor drag and landing brake, reach and hilt heights, anchor pull-back and swing follow-through. Values persist per machine in `user://sword_tweaks.cfg`, control local motion (the host reconstructs the blade for raider contact), and **Print values** writes the current set to the console for copying into the defaults. Existing saved values still load, except the old launch-strength `strike_turn`, which is ignored since cut speed replaced it; use **Defaults** to evaluate the current baseline. The underlying `follow_spring` setting controls gesture force. Saved values remain compatible; arc and elevation limits are fixed to keep high settings from causing runaway motion.

## Limits

Damage still uses the existing host-owned melee cone and cover checks; this is not blade-mesh collision. Angular inertia, body reaction and ground contact are a stylized procedural approximation, not a ragdoll or free rigid-body weapon. The exaggerated pickup and bounded arc deliberately prioritize readability and control. The host's approved swing window is still 0.52 s (windup, active, recovery), and the cone hit at 0.15 s meets a tip still building speed (about 8 m/s). A level cut is only about 145 degrees round when that window ends, so blade contact keeps the swing's damage while the host's own reconstruction of the stroke is still in the air (`CombatController.cuts`, `HeavySwordMotion.stroke_live`): it lands about 0.68 s after the swing near 185-190 degrees, and the follow-through is capped at 0.85 s after the host approved the swing. Only an approved melee swing starts a live stroke; held drag input can steer the blade but never extends damage past the landing or the cap. The follow-through keeps the swing's one hit per target, does not change the attack cooldown or stamina, and ends on guard, dash, stagger (including a parry), respawn or a match freeze. After the landing the sliding blade only shoves. The blade can intersect walls/props. Foot targets follow ground height but do not move the collision capsule onto steps, solve moving-platform adhesion, or guarantee perfect contact on extreme terrain. No physics body or collision shape is scaled.

## Verification

```sh
"$GODOT" --headless --editor --path . --quit
python3 scripts/smoke_test.py "$GODOT"
"$GODOT" --headless --path . res://tests/sword_test.tscn
"$GODOT" --headless --path . res://tests/inertia_test.tscn
"$GODOT" --path . res://tests/sword_test.tscn
```

Checks cover gesture rearming, request ownership/validation/cooldown, prediction rejection, both hand grips, camera movement during drag (visible run), a blade that cannot be held up, arm stretch and anchor drag with owner resistance, the body facing the blade, a mouse-driven swing, a level cut that sweeps at least 170 degrees, a cut that still turns about half a turn relative to a camera that turned with the drag, walking onto a planted blade without the hilt passing the hips, with tip speed building up to 7–15 m/s mid-swing instead of peaking at launch (30/60/120 Hz), left/right carry-through without reversal while held or released at 30/60/120 Hz, overhead lift, persistent landings and return cuts, bounded held-input arcs, coupled owner movement without spirals, rapid alternating cuts, release settling, moving-gesture consistency at 30/60/120 Hz, bounded owner displacement, correction reset, and planted-foot targets on both rigs. Optional network mode uses two test-scene processes: `-- --sword-network --host --port=7788`, then `-- --sword-network --join=127.0.0.1 --port=7788`. Stop the host after the client reports `SWORD_NETWORK_OK`; this also checks host reconstruction of continuous aim and held intent.

Rendered poses and automated visible input checks are separate from manual acceptance. Trackpad feel, live camera readability, contact polish and two-Mac Tailscale play still need human testing. No latency, bandwidth or thermal measurements are claimed.

Implementation follows Godot's [SkeletonModifier3D callback and coordinate-space contract](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonmodifier3d.html).
