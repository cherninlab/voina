# Possessed villagers at the church

The graveyard in front of the hillside church north of the village (`ChurchSite`, yard centre (-4, -103), 16 m ring) is haunted by four possessed villagers. They are on by default: the lobby switch **Possessed villagers at the church** starts ticked, and turning it off before **Host game** gives a church yard without them. Joining players receive the host's villagers automatically, including a late join. In Terminal:

```sh
"$GODOT" --path . -- --host            # villagers at the church (default)
"$GODOT" --path . -- --host --no-pve   # an empty church yard
# A dedicated server haunts the yard too; --pve still forces it on.
"$GODOT" --headless --path . -- --server --port=7777
# Other players join normally; they need no flag.
"$GODOT" --path . -- --join=your-host-magicdns-name
```

They are the first enemies in the game and a light threat: they never hunt anyone across the map and never follow a player out of the yard. How their bodies look possessed (proportions, joints, crawl and pounce poses) is described in [possessed-villagers.md](possessed-villagers.md).

## Behaviour

Each villager has **60 health** and haunts the grave it rose from (`HostileMob`, host only):

- **Lingering.** It stands still for long pauses of 3 to 9 seconds, sometimes jerks its head and shoulders round to a new direction, and otherwise wanders slowly (0.8 m/s) to a spot within 3.5 m of its grave.
- **Territory.** It only cares about players within 3 m of the yard ring (19 m from the yard centre). Anyone farther away is ignored, even right next to a villager standing at the edge.
- **Staring.** A visible player within **14 m** makes it freeze and turn to face them for 1 to 3 seconds. While the player stays beyond 8 m it keeps watching, turning to follow them; a player who lingers inside the ring is approached anyway after 6 to 10 seconds of staring.
- **Creeping.** Closer than **8 m** it creeps in, mostly on all fours (`CRAWL`, 2.5 m/s, seven times in ten) and otherwise in an upright shamble (`MOVE`, 1.2 m/s). Each gait lasts 1.5 to 3.5 seconds, and three times in ten it stops dead for 0.3 to 0.9 seconds first. It spirals in toward a point 3.5 m beside the player rather than walking straight at them.
- **Pouncing.** Within **4.5 m**, with its cooldown ready, sight and a clear line between the two bodies, and the player at most 1.5 m outside the ring, it coils for **0.5 seconds** (`ACT`, the telegraph, turning to follow the player) and then really jumps (`LEAP`): 4.8 m/s up and 6 to 8 m/s toward where the player stood at launch, about half a second in the air and 0.6 m high. Touching the player (1.1 m, centre to centre, in front of it) deals **8 damage once per leap** through the player's ordinary `receive_hit`: a held front guard takes 2 health and 20 stamina, and a parry staggers the villager out of the air and back. It lands, stays crouched for 0.7 seconds, and waits **2.5 to 4 seconds** (4 after a parry) before the next pounce, circling at about 3.5 m meanwhile. Dashing aside after the launch makes it miss.
- **Provoked.** A player who hurts a villager from inside the territory makes it bolder for **8 seconds**: no stare, it creeps from 14 m and always crawls. A hit from outside the territory provokes nothing.
- **Losing a player.** When a player leaves the territory or stays hidden for 3 seconds, the villager stares after the last place it saw them for 1.5 to 3 seconds, then shambles back to its grave.
- **Never leaving.** Every movement goal is clamped to 15.5 m from the yard centre; a villager carried outside by a shove or a leap walks home.

There are six graves (offsets in `MobSystem.SPAWN_SPOTS`); the first four are the opening places. Once a corpse is removed, a replacement rises 5 seconds later at the grave farthest from the players, so the yard always holds four villagers; there are no waves.

Player melee, beam and swept bolts all damage them through the existing combat paths. The player's simulated longsword also touches them directly: the host sweeps the blade segment against each villager's capsule every tick, and any contact faster than 1 m/s shoves the villager along the blade's travel, flexing the struck part of its body, with a splash of blood, hopping above 3 m/s. During an approved swing the same contact deals the swing's 25 damage once per target, and above 7 m/s at the touching point, or whenever the blow is lethal, it bisects the villager into two tumbling halves along the blade's plane: each half ragdolls every part with skin on its side (a severed forearm falls as its own piece), its opening is capped with a solid flesh face, the cut bursts blood both ways along the blade's plane and each half pours a heavy stream from the middle of its stump (not the skin the blade touched) that splatters while it tumbles, then pools where the stream lands; more blood landing on a pool feeds and spreads it as one surface instead of stacking a new edge on it. A swing whose cone reaches a villager before the blade geometrically touches it resolves through the same path with the blade's current motion, so every damaging swing gets the same feedback. Each damaging hit freezes the villager's animation for 70 ms and paints a wide, ragged, wet gash where the blade's plane crosses the body (riding on the nearest bone, up to six per villager) with blood running down from it. Blood (`gameplay/mobs/hit_effects.gd`, one shared colour for drops, splatter, wounds and cut flesh) sprays in a fan within the blade's plane, flicks off along the rest of the swing as a trail of drops, sheets off the gash and mists at the contact; where it lands on static ground or walls it leaves directional splatter, and a pool spreads under the villager. Splatter and pools are liquid, not paint: each mark stores thickness and ends in a crisp edge where it thins out; the surface is a flat mirror where the blood is deep and turns bumpy toward the edge, where it is only a film over uneven ground, so reflections and the sun's glint break up into glitter there (a short screen-space march reflects bodies and props, the sky elsewhere), dulling and darkening slowly as it dries. A mark is cut off per pixel wherever nothing solid lies right behind it, so splatter on a crate stops at the crate's edge instead of hanging in the air, and a pool runs under objects rather than climbing them. A splatter's impact sits on the spot where the blood landed, with drops trailing ahead along its travel; a pool floods outward from where it started, and drops that land in a pool (or are covered by one) join it. They are drawn as meshes laid over the surface (the Mobile renderer shows only 8 decals per mesh, and the terrain is one mesh), fades after 25 s, at most 96 marks at once. Players walking into a villager push it along as well. Damage shares the swing's one-hit-per-target rule with the cone, so a swing never hits the same villager twice. A dead villager's host body is removed after 1.3 seconds while its ragdoll lies for 8 seconds.

Villager bodies are procedural on every peer, driven only by the host's existing reaction RPCs and health, with no new authority. Foot planting runs over every clip: a `MobFeet` modifier raycasts the static ground under each animated foot, keeps the foot's height above that ground, drops the hips so the lower foot reaches, lets an engine `TwoBoneIK3D` bend the legs, and then tilts each foot onto the slope, so villagers stand, jog and attack with planted feet on slopes and stones. Hits flex the body like a puppet. `MobPuppet` keeps underdamped angular springs on the pelvis, spine, neck, head and arms, layered over the animation. A blow spins every spring bone between the pelvis and the struck point by the torque the blade's travel exerts about that joint, the head and the other arm lag the other way, and everything swings back within about a second while the feet stay planted. Wounds follow the final, flexed pose. A death becomes a full ragdoll: the villager's own visual moves into the scene, pauses its clip and simulates fifteen jointed `PhysicalBone3D` parts (cone joints at the spine, hips, shoulders and neck, hinges at the knees and elbows) under a `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D`. It falls along the latest shove, lands on the terrain and stays for 8 seconds, outliving the villager node. A bisect bakes each vertex's distance to the cut plane into the mesh in the pose of the cut, then gives each half a ragdoll of the parts on its side, with blood pulsing from the cut face for 1.4 seconds. Parts whose jointed parent was cut away fall free, such as a forearm hanging below a waist cut, and bones left without a body ride on the nearest simulated part, so the cut edge stays on the body while the halves crumple. Ragdoll parts sit on collision layer 0 with mask 1: they land on the world and get shoved by actors, but block nobody and never collide with each other. `tests/mob_body_test.tscn` checks, on the male and then the female body, planting on a 20 degree slope, the hip drop, flex and settle, the death ragdoll outliving its villager, and the bisect splitting the parts between two jointed halves. Run it at a fixed frame rate, as `scripts/mob_smoke_test.py` does: `"$GODOT" --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 res://tests/mob_body_test.tscn`. `scripts/capture_mob_body.gd` renders staged frames: `"$GODOT" --path . -s scripts/capture_mob_body.gd -- --capture-dir=/tmp/mob_body`; add `--model=male` or `--model=female` to stage only that body, and `--bisect-only` for just the cuts.

Each villager is the already imported CC0 Quaternius male or female model with its own retargeted animation library. The choice is `HostileMob.is_female(mob_id)`, a hash of the id the spawner replicates, so every peer and late joiner shows the same body without a new synchronized property; ids 1 to 4 of the opening spawn give female, male, female, male. Both models share one 65-bone skeleton with the same bone names and the same three skinned meshes (body, eyes, eyebrows), so feet, puppet springs, ragdoll parts, wounds and the bisect bake run unchanged on either. No additional asset pack or license is needed. Model and animation provenance remain in `content/characters/quaternius/` and `content/animations/quaternius/`.

A different model works with this pipeline once it has, in Godot: a `Skeleton3D` using the bone names in `mob_feet.gd` (`thigh/calf/foot/ball_l/r`, `pelvis`), `mob_puppet.gd` (`spine_01`–`spine_03`, `neck_01`, `Head`, `upperarm/lowerarm_l/r`) and `MobRagdoll.PARTS` (also `hand_l/r`), with a humanoid rest pose about 1.8 m tall, feet at the origin and facing +Z before the visual's 180 degree turn; an `AnimationLibrary` with `Idle`, `Jog_Fwd`, `Punch_Jab` and `Hit_Chest` retargeted to that skeleton with tracks under `Model/Armature/Skeleton3D` (as `scripts/build_character_animations.gd` builds for the Quaternius bodies); and skinned, indexed triangle meshes, each with a `Skin`, 4 or 8 weights per vertex, and `UV2` and `TANGENT` free to overwrite (the cut bakes each vertex's plane distance into `UV2.x` and the plane normal into `TANGENT`), with `StandardMaterial3D` albedo textures that the cut shader can copy. Closed, watertight meshes cap best: the cap is built from the edges where the plane crosses the skin.

## Ownership and replication

Peer 1 owns villager spawning, movement, perception, pounce timing, health and death. Clients display synchronized snapshots at 30 Hz (`sync_position`, `yaw`, `state`, `attack_age`, health and combat phase); there is no client mob-damage or mob-spawn request RPC. Villager bodies expose the same combat interface as players, so bolts, block, parry and poise reuse the current rules. Player swing hit IDs use negative mob IDs because every mob has the same server authority.

`HostileMob.State` is the contract with the bodies, whose visual receives the raw state each frame (`show_state(state, health, staggered, windup)`, `windup` is `ACT`). New states are only appended, because the integer is replicated:

| State | Meaning | `attack_age` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `IDLE` 0 | standing, lingering; also the crouch after landing | – |
| `MOVE` 1 | upright slow shamble (wandering, returning home, some approaches) | – |
| `ACT` 2 | pounce coil, the telegraph | 0 to 0.5 s |
| `DEAD` 3 | dead, ragdoll | – |
| `STARE` 4 | frozen, facing a player (`yaw` follows them) | – |
| `CRAWL` 5 | moving on all fours | – |
| `LEAP` 6 | airborne pounce | from launch |

The brain separates perception from action. It checks sight every 0.2 seconds; static cover blocks sight, and a hidden player's last seen position is remembered for 3 seconds. Movement never reads a hidden player's position. Stagger cancels a coil or a leap.

## Navigation

The yard is a slope (ground from about 1.4 m to 5 m), so `MobNavigation` keeps the ground height of every cell of an `AStarGrid2D` covering 44 by 44 m around the yard centre at 0.5 m (89 by 89 cells). When the encounter starts the host samples the static collision once, in about 20 ms: a ray finds the lowest surface under each cell (a ray stopped by a roof, branch or gravestone top keeps going down from just below it), a capsule wider than the villager's checks clearance from 0.35 m above that ground, and neighbouring cells more than 0.3 m apart in height are both closed, which cuts off ledges and tomb tops. Path points stand 0.95 m above their cell's ground. `nearest()` searches outward ring by ring from the cell under a point, so open ground costs one lookup. Routes refresh every 0.4 seconds; physics keeps bodies out of actors and walls.

Limits: collision that appears after the encounter starts is not seen (the church and graveyard collision must exist when hosting begins), there is no dynamic obstacle rebuilding, crowd avoidance or multi-floor pathing, and the leap is not routed: a pounce only needs a clear line. The collision capsule stays an upright 1.8 m capsule while the body crawls, so player hits, shoves and blade contact treat a crawling villager as standing.

Design references: Jeff Orkin's [Three States and a Plan: The A.I. of F.E.A.R.](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf), GDC 2006, informed the separation of goal choice from small execution states. The supplied *Cunning Lords: Tactical AI and Troop Control for Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord* dissertation (Joaquim Quadrado, October 2021, `86455-Joaquim-Quadrado-dissertacao.pdf`) informed the choice to reuse existing engine and combat systems. This starter does not implement GOAP, squad tactics or Bannerlord troop control. The papers are references, not imported code or bundled assets.

## Verification

```sh
"$GODOT" --headless --editor --path . --quit
python3 scripts/mob_smoke_test.py "$GODOT"
python3 scripts/smoke_test.py "$GODOT"
```

`tests/mob_test.tscn` builds a 6 degree slope around the yard centre with a cover wall and a roof. Run it at a fixed frame rate, as `scripts/mob_smoke_test.py` does: `"$GODOT" --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 res://tests/mob_test.tscn`. It checks that the grid follows the slope, detours around cover and sees the ground under a roof; that villagers stay home while a player passes outside the yard; the stare, watching beyond 8 m, cover and memory; a natural stare, creep, coil and leap with one 8-damage hit; the coil's reaction time, the real jump and the cooldown; block and parry against a leap; provoking from inside but not outside the yard; per-mob melee deduplication, beam and bolt damage, death and corpse removal; staring after a leaving player and returning home; and spawn heights on the slope plus a replacement at the farthest grave. A real ENet host then lets a villager pounce on its player before a client joins; the client verifies every villager, current health, position and state, replicated damage to a player, refusal of local client damage and spawn attempts, and host-driven death and despawn. CI runs both suites. Two-Mac Tailscale play and human animation and input acceptance remain manual checks.

## Future environmental creatures

Passive ambient animals are a separate future slice: wandering within an area, idling, fleeing nearby players and returning home, with suitable licensed creature models and clips. They should share only the ownership/spawning conventions they need. A full planner, wildlife combat, loot and ecosystem simulation are not part of this encounter.
