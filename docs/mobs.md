# Optional PvE raiders

Enable **PvE · 3 hostile raiders** before choosing **Host game**. Joining players receive the host's encounter automatically, including a late join. PvP remains the default when the switch is off. In Terminal:

```sh
"$GODOT" --path . -- --host --pve
# Dedicated server also supports the encounter:
"$GODOT" --headless --path . -- --server --pve --port=7777
# Other players join normally; they do not need --pve.
"$GODOT" --path . -- --join=your-host-magicdns-name
```

There are three humanoid raiders with **60 health** each, in the models' own colours. They approach visible players, stop to attack, and their name label turns yellow during a **0.5 second windup** (the body itself is not tinted or flashed). Each committed attack deals **12 melee damage**, followed by **0.8 seconds recovery**. Move out of the attack cone, hold a front guard for reduced damage, or time a parry to stagger the raider. Player melee, beam and swept bolts all damage them through the existing combat paths. The player's simulated longsword also touches them directly: the host sweeps the blade segment against each raider's capsule every tick, and any contact faster than 1 m/s shoves the raider along the blade's travel, flexing the struck part of its body, with a splash of blood, hopping above 3 m/s. During an approved swing the same contact deals the swing's 25 damage once per target, and above 7 m/s at the touching point, or whenever the blow is lethal, it bisects the raider into two tumbling halves along the blade's plane: each half ragdolls every part with skin on its side (a severed forearm falls as its own piece), its opening is capped with a solid flesh face, the cut bursts blood both ways along the blade's plane and each half pours out a heavy stream that trails splatter and spreads a wide pool where it lands. A swing whose cone reaches a raider before the blade geometrically touches it resolves through the same path with the blade's current motion, so every damaging swing gets the same feedback. Each damaging hit freezes the raider's animation for 70 ms and paints a wide, ragged, wet gash where the blade's plane crosses the body (riding on the nearest bone, up to six per raider) with blood running down from it. Blood (`gameplay/mobs/hit_effects.gd`, one shared colour for drops, splatter, wounds and cut flesh) sprays in a fan within the blade's plane, flicks off along the rest of the swing as a trail of drops, sheets off the gash and mists at the contact; where it lands on static ground or walls it leaves directional splatter, and a pool spreads under the raider. Splatter is drawn as meshes laid over the surface (the Mobile renderer shows only 8 decals per mesh, and the terrain is one mesh), fades after 25 s, at most 96 marks at once. Players walking into a raider push it along as well. Damage shares the swing's one-hit-per-target rule with the cone, so a swing never hits the same raider twice. A dead raider's host body is removed after 1.3 seconds while its ragdoll lies for 8 seconds, and a replacement spawns 5 seconds later at the spawn point farthest from the players, so the encounter keeps three raiders in rotation; there are no waves.

Raider bodies are procedural on every peer, driven only by the host's existing reaction RPCs and health, with no new authority. Foot planting runs over every clip: a `MobFeet` modifier raycasts the static ground under each animated foot, keeps the foot's height above that ground, drops the hips so the lower foot reaches, lets an engine `TwoBoneIK3D` bend the legs, and then tilts each foot onto the slope, so raiders stand, jog and attack with planted feet on slopes and stones. Hits flex the body like a puppet. `MobPuppet` keeps underdamped angular springs on the pelvis, spine, neck, head and arms, layered over the animation. A blow spins every spring bone between the pelvis and the struck point by the torque the blade's travel exerts about that joint, the head and the other arm lag the other way, and everything swings back within about a second while the feet stay planted. Wounds follow the final, flexed pose. A death becomes a full ragdoll: the raider's own visual moves into the scene, pauses its clip and simulates fifteen jointed `PhysicalBone3D` parts (cone joints at the spine, hips, shoulders and neck, hinges at the knees and elbows) under a `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D`. It falls along the latest shove, lands on the terrain and stays for 8 seconds, outliving the raider node. A bisect bakes each vertex's distance to the cut plane into the mesh in the pose of the cut, then gives each half a ragdoll of the parts on its side, with blood pulsing from the cut face for 1.4 seconds. Parts whose jointed parent was cut away fall free, such as a forearm hanging below a waist cut, and bones left without a body ride on the nearest simulated part, so the cut edge stays on the body while the halves crumple. Ragdoll parts sit on collision layer 0 with mask 1: they land on the world and get shoved by actors, but block nobody and never collide with each other. `tests/mob_body_test.tscn` checks, on the male and then the female body, planting on a 20 degree slope, the hip drop, flex and settle, the death ragdoll outliving its raider, and the bisect splitting the parts between two jointed halves. Run it at a fixed frame rate, as `scripts/mob_smoke_test.py` does: `"$GODOT" --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 res://tests/mob_body_test.tscn`. `scripts/capture_mob_body.gd` renders staged frames: `"$GODOT" --path . -s scripts/capture_mob_body.gd -- --capture-dir=/tmp/mob_body`; add `--model=male` or `--model=female` to stage only that body, and `--bisect-only` for just the cuts.

Each raider is the already imported CC0 Quaternius male or female model with its own retargeted animation library. The choice is `HostileMob.is_female(mob_id)`, a hash of the id the spawner replicates, so every peer and late joiner shows the same body without a new synchronized property; ids 1 to 3 of the first wave give female, male, female. Both models share one 65-bone skeleton with the same bone names and the same three skinned meshes (body, eyes, eyebrows), so feet, puppet springs, ragdoll parts, wounds and the bisect bake run unchanged on either. No additional asset pack or license is needed. Model and animation provenance remain in `content/characters/quaternius/` and `content/animations/quaternius/`.

A different model works with this pipeline once it has, in Godot: a `Skeleton3D` using the bone names in `mob_feet.gd` (`thigh/calf/foot/ball_l/r`, `pelvis`), `mob_puppet.gd` (`spine_01`–`spine_03`, `neck_01`, `Head`, `upperarm/lowerarm_l/r`) and `MobRagdoll.PARTS` (also `hand_l/r`), with a humanoid rest pose about 1.8 m tall, feet at the origin and facing +Z before the visual's 180 degree turn; an `AnimationLibrary` with `Idle`, `Jog_Fwd`, `Punch_Jab` and `Hit_Chest` retargeted to that skeleton with tracks under `Model/Armature/Skeleton3D` (as `scripts/build_character_animations.gd` builds for the Quaternius bodies); and skinned, indexed triangle meshes, each with a `Skin`, 4 or 8 weights per vertex, and `UV2` and `TANGENT` free to overwrite (the cut bakes each vertex's plane distance into `UV2.x` and the plane normal into `TANGENT`), with `StandardMaterial3D` albedo textures that the cut shader can copy. Closed, watertight meshes cap best: the cap is built from the edges where the plane crosses the skin.

## Ownership and behavior

Peer 1 owns mob spawning, movement, perception, attack timing, health and death. Clients display synchronized snapshots; there is no client mob-damage or mob-spawn request RPC. Mob bodies expose the same combat interface as players, so bolts, block, parry and poise reuse the current rules. Player swing hit IDs use negative mob IDs because every mob has the same server authority.

The brain separates perception from action execution: idle, move and act, plus a dead state. It checks sight every 0.2 seconds within 14 metres. Static cover blocks sight; a hidden player's last seen position is remembered for three seconds. An attack commits its facing at the start of windup, then checks range, cone and cover again at impact. Stagger cancels the action.

Godot's `AStarGrid2D` provides ground-plane paths on a shared 0.5 metre grid. The host samples static collisions once when the encounter starts with clearance wider than the mob capsule. Routes refresh every 0.4 seconds; physics prevents bodies passing through actors and walls. This is for the current flat arena: no jumping, climbing, multi-floor paths, dynamic obstacle rebuilding or sophisticated crowd avoidance. Three actors are the intended first slice.

Design references: Jeff Orkin's [Three States and a Plan: The A.I. of F.E.A.R.](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf), GDC 2006, informed the separation of goal choice from small execution states. The supplied *Cunning Lords: Tactical AI and Troop Control for Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord* dissertation (Joaquim Quadrado, October 2021, `86455-Joaquim-Quadrado-dissertacao.pdf`) informed the choice to reuse existing engine and combat systems. This starter does not implement GOAP, squad tactics or Bannerlord troop control. The papers are references, not imported code or bundled assets.

## Verification

```sh
"$GODOT" --headless --editor --path . --quit
python3 scripts/mob_smoke_test.py "$GODOT"
python3 scripts/smoke_test.py "$GODOT"
```

The focused scene checks pathfinding around cover, sight and memory, actual pursuit, windup, one hit per attack, player block/parry, committed-facing misses, per-mob melee deduplication, beam and bolt damage, death and corpse removal. A real ENet host then runs its mobs before a client joins; the client verifies current positions and health, replicated damage to a player, refusal of local client damage/spawn attempts, and host-driven death/despawn. CI runs both suites. Two-Mac Tailscale play and human animation/input acceptance remain manual checks.

## Future environmental creatures

Passive ambient animals are a separate future slice: wandering within an area, idling, fleeing nearby players and returning home, with suitable licensed creature models and clips. They should share only the ownership/spawning conventions they need. A full planner, wildlife combat, loot and ecosystem simulation are not part of this encounter.
